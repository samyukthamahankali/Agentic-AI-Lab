CYBERSECURITY KNOWLEDGE BASE - INCIDENT RESPONSE

Incident response is the organized process used to detect, investigate,
contain, eradicate, and recover from cybersecurity incidents.

A typical incident-response lifecycle includes:

1. Preparation
Establish policies, procedures, tools, communication channels, and response
teams before incidents occur.

2. Detection and Analysis
Identify suspicious activity and determine whether a security incident has
occurred.

3. Containment
Limit the impact and prevent the incident from spreading.

4. Eradication
Remove malware, compromised accounts, malicious persistence mechanisms, and
other causes of the incident.

5. Recovery
Restore affected systems and services to normal operation while monitoring
for additional malicious activity.

6. Lessons Learned
Document the incident, identify root causes, and improve security controls.

Important incident-response principles:
- Preserve evidence.
- Record important actions and timestamps.
- Avoid unnecessary modification of affected systems.
- Follow organizational procedures.
- Escalate incidents according to severity.
- Communicate through approved channels.

For a suspected compromised endpoint:
1. Isolate the endpoint from the network.
2. Notify the incident-response team.
3. Preserve relevant evidence.
4. Review endpoint and authentication logs.
5. Identify suspicious processes and connections.
6. Determine the scope of compromise.
7. Remove the threat using approved procedures.
8. Reset compromised credentials when appropriate.
9. Restore the system from a trusted state.
10. Monitor the endpoint after recovery.

High-severity incidents may include:
- Ransomware outbreaks.
- Large-scale credential compromise.
- Data breaches.
- Active unauthorized access.
- Major service disruption.

Incident response should prioritize safety, containment, evidence preservation,
and controlled recovery.
